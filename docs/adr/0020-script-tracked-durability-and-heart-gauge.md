# Script-tracked Durability behind a one-row heart gauge

The HUD draws a ridden mob's health as mount hearts, and no entity component turns that off. At 1000 health the truck filled ten rows of the rider's view beside the crosshair (1.0.14 release evidence). So `minecraft:health` is now a gauge of 20, one row of 10 hearts, and the truck's real Durability of 1000 points is kept by the script in the `blake:durability` dynamic property.

Lowering health alone does not work. With health 20 and every damage multiplier scaled by 20/1000, a Game run measured a 0.2-damage hit taking a full point (run b693932c): each hit costs at least 1 health, so the truck would die in 20 hits. The before-hurt handler therefore charges every hit on a truck to its Durability. `durability.js` owns the 75% combat, projectile and explosion reduction and the fall immunity, which the `damage_sensor` multipliers used to do. A hit the truck survives is cancelled, and the gauge is set to the share of Durability left. Because the engine applies no damage, it opens no immunity window, so the script copies the engine's 10-tick window: a repeat hit costs only what it exceeds the last one by. Without that, lava would cost every tick. A player's blow that uses up the Durability is still a deliberate retrieval that returns the Vehicle Item. From anything else, the handler raises the blow so the engine kills the truck through its gauge, with the original cause, and the loot table drops Scrap.

Hiding the mount hearts while seated was also prototyped (`HudElement.HorseHealth` through the Script API). The maintainer chose the gauge, so riders keep a damage reading. Costs: a hit the truck survives is cancelled, so it should make no hurt flash or sound, and a truck placed before this change counts as full the first time it is hit. Approved by the maintainer on 2026-10-19.
