# Script-driven steering and handbrake

The add-on now drives the truck itself instead of letting the engine do it: `minecraft:input_ground_controlled` is removed, and each tick the Script API reads the Driver Seat's movement vector and held Jump button, then sets the truck's yaw and horizontal velocity. W/S throttle, brake, and reverse; A/D turn the front wheels (Coordinated Four-Wheel Steering), and the truck turns only while rolling, through a speed-dependent bicycle model; holding Space is the Handbrake Drift; the driver's mouse only moves the camera. We chose this because engine ground control steers by the rider's look direction, strafes on A/D, and treats Spacebar as a dismount, so every Suspension Jump briefly ejected the driver on the client (#32) and steering could not be tied to the keys. Native jump components (`minecraft:can_power_jump`) would have kept the Spacebar but not fixed steering, and scripts can read only the Jump and Sneak buttons, so the Suspension Jump is retired rather than rebound; Crush Stomp now fires when the truck lands from a drop of 3 or more blocks. Because the engine applies ground friction (0.91 x block slipperiness x `minecraft:friction_modifier`), liquid drag, or air drag before moving the truck, driving divides its velocity by the measured retention so the truck covers its intended speed on every surface; a short window after a Shoreline Step-Up keeps pushing the truck over the bank's edge, which engine ground control used to do from the rider's input mid-air. Tuning (top speed 1.1 blocks/tick, about 1.5 s to reach it, about 2 s to coast to a stop, reverse at a third of top speed) lives in `driving.js`. Scenario Runs verify steering, the handbrake, and per-tick rider retention through the `driverInput` seam; whether the client reports A as +x on the movement vector and whether Space no longer dismounts can only be confirmed in the real client.
